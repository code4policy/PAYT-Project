According to data from the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection's 2024 Municipal Solid Waste and Recycling Survey, 322,000 households participated in Boston's municipal trash program. Over 5 years since implementing Pay as You Throw, the 5 municipalities that introduced PAYT (for which we have data) reduced their solid waste disposal by an average of 0.2600936 tons per household. If Boston were to reduce its solid waste disposal by the same amount, over 5 years, each household would reduce their trash disposal from 0.57858695652 tons to 0.31849335652 tons per household, or a reduction of 44.95%. Multiplying 0.2600936 tons by Boston's 322,000 participating households results in an estimated reduction of 83,629.87 tons of trash per household over 5 years, or 16,725.974 tons per household per year in Boston. At a tipping fee of $103.38 per ton, this reduction translates to an estimated savings of $8,646,580.35 over 5 years, or $1,729,131.19 annually.
